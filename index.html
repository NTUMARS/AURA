Abstract

Humans and animals act adaptively. Under uncertainty they recruit less habit, think longer, and move cautiously, while dispatching fast routines when the situation is familiar. Current robot policies, by contrast, are rigid.

Whether the moment is familiar or uncertain, they always infer from the same span of observation, for the same amount of time, to produce the same length of execution, which limits their flexibility in the complex, ever-changing real world. This paper presents the AURA policy, enabling a robot to judge how action-uncertain each moment is and adapt its behavior accordingly.

We first reveal that the initial noise of prevalent generative policies should scale adaptively with the diversity of expert actions, rather than being fixed. This inferred noise, large where valid actions diverge and small where they coincide, subsequently doubles as a measure of action uncertainty that schedules robot behaviors. Mirroring biology, higher uncertainty elicits shorter recent memory, longer inference, and more cautious execution, and vice versa.

Across extensive simulated and real-world tasks, and comparisons with 12 state-of-the-art baselines, AURA learns faster, sustains multimodality far more stably, reduces inference time dramatically, and especially enables the emergence of novel, combinatorial behaviors never seen during training without scaling. The same adaptivity that makes a policy efficient also makes it creative.

How it works

One inferred uncertainty, three adaptive behaviors

AURA keeps the flow-policy backbone and changes one thing: where generation starts. Instead of a fixed Gaussian, a lightweight stochastic inference network (ScoNet) predicts, at every step, how much noise the current context warrants. Generation is anchored on the recent action history and noise is injected only where the demonstrations are genuinely diverse.

The source is a mixture, not a Gaussian. For each action dimension, generation starts from

x0=(1 − w)⊙h+w⊙ε

where h is the recent action history, ε is Gaussian noise and w ∈ [0, 1] is ScoNet's per-dimension weight. With w = 0 the policy is the deterministic, history-anchored A2A; with w = 1 it is standard flow matching. AURA lives in between and moves along that axis from moment to moment.

Three losses calibrate the weight end-to-end. The flow-matching loss rewards a source the velocity field can transport cleanly. A reconstruction loss, gated by (1 − w) with a stop-gradient, encourages single-step generation on near-deterministic dimensions. A diversity loss supplies the opposing pressure: it retrieves the K nearest demonstrations in feature space and penalizes any source narrower than the spread of their futures. At equilibrium w settles at the smallest noise that still covers the experts' diversity, and a theorem shows this condition holds for every network capacity.

Under high uncertainty the policy conditions on a shorter window of past frames, so it is not locked into the route it arrived on. Under low uncertainty it keeps the full history.

Inference schedulew̄ ↑ → think longer

Think longer

The number of denoising steps scales with the uncertainty, at least one and at most a budget Imax. Because most control steps are near-deterministic, the budget is dominated by one-step generation.

k = max(1, ⌈w̄ · Imax⌉)

Execution schedulew̄ ↑ → act cautiously

Act more cautiously

Fewer predicted actions are committed before re-planning when the moment is uncertain; when it is familiar, the policy executes a long chunk open-loop and moves fast.

§ 01 · High learning efficiency

AURA learns with striking efficiency

Anchoring the source on the recent action history places generation near its target rather than at a distant Gaussian, and injecting only as much noise as the local expert spread requires shrinks the space the policy must learn to map from. Both reduce the complexity of the source-to-action mapping, an advantage unavailable to policies that start from a fixed, unnecessarily broad prior.

Ahead at every budget, most visibly early. On the visuomotor benchmark AURA stays ahead of all 9 baselines at most training budgets, and the advantage is largest when only a few gradient steps have been taken and making each one count matters most. On the π0.5 backbone it matches the vanilla policy's final success with roughly 1.3× fewer training steps, attaining the highest average success on both Libero Object (85.5 %) and Libero Goal (92.5 %) after 1000 steps.

Why cautious behavior also raises success. The data itself induces varying learning difficulty. The schedules allocate the policy's effort in proportion to the local difficulty of the learning problem, spending caution only where the data made the mapping hard. That AURA outperforms AURA0, which shares the adaptive noise but keeps behaviors fixed, isolates the contribution of the schedules themselves.

§ 02 · Stable multimodality

Multimodality only when it matters

Multiple valid solutions to one problem. Unlike deterministic policies, AURA keeps its learning efficiency without giving them up: it stays decisive within each mode and turns stochastic only at the branching moment between them, sustaining multimodality far more stably than a fully noised flow policy, with a 65 % average gain on real-world tasks.

§ 03 · Fast and smooth inference

A single step where nothing branches, and smooth everywhere

Because AURA adapts its number of denoising steps to context, it collapses generation to one step on near-deterministic phases, where conventional generative policies still spend ten to a hundred. Anchoring on the action history keeps successive chunks continuous at their boundaries, without any heuristic that stitches discontinuities.

§ 04 · Emergence of novel modes

Behaviors that never appear in the demonstrations

No skill primitives, no scaling, no external planner. At the uncertain moments between sub-tasks AURA deliberately looks back less and stays exploratory, so it can freely stitch together pieces of motion learned in different demonstrations. The outcome is behavior never demonstrated: assembling a puzzle, tidying a table, or cooking in an order no expert ever showed.

We use the word emergence in a deliberately narrow sense. AURA acquires no new skill and solves no new task. It solves a known task in ways no expert demonstrated, recombining demonstrated segments at whatever granularity the inferred uncertainty rises, from whole sub-tasks down to fragments of a single action. Such emergence is therefore interpretable rather than opaque. With adaptive uncertainty, a policy can be made more flexible by drawing more fully on the diversity the data already contains, rather than by collecting more of it.

Jigsaw Puzzle Franka FR3

Four colored pieces must be moved from an initial layout to a target configuration, a long-horizon task whose sub-assemblies can be ordered in many valid ways. The 100 demonstrations contain only two orders. AURA reproduces both and composes two more at test time: one arises because the two demonstrated orders pass through a briefly identical observation after their second placement, so by shortening its history and re-sampling there AURA is not locked into the route it arrived on; in the other the puzzle is completed in three steps, because placing one piece incidentally carries a neighbouring one into position. Platforms

Two real robots, both control spaces

Experiments span joint-space and task-space control under both absolute and incremental action modes, plus RoboVerse and Libero in simulation. The π0.5 backbone serves only to show that the adaptive mechanism scales; every behavior-emergence result comes from lightweight visuomotor policies trained from scratch.
