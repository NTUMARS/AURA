How it works

One inferred uncertainty, three adaptive behaviors

AURA keeps the flow-policy backbone and changes one thing: where generation starts. A lightweight stochastic inference network (ScoNet) predicts, at every step, how much noise the current context warrants.

The source is a mixture, not a Gaussian. For each action dimension, generation starts from

x0=(1 − w)⊙h+w⊙ε

where h is the recent action history, ε is Gaussian noise and w ∈ [0, 1] is ScoNet's per-dimension weight. With w = 0 the policy is the deterministic, history-anchored A2A; with w = 1 it is standard flow matching. AURA lives in between and moves along that axis from moment to moment.

Three losses calibrate the weight end-to-end. The flow-matching loss rewards a source the velocity field can transport cleanly. A reconstruction loss, gated by (1 − w) with a stop-gradient, encourages single-step generation on near-deterministic dimensions. A diversity loss supplies the opposing pressure: it retrieves the K nearest demonstrations in feature space and penalizes any source narrower than the spread of their futures. At equilibrium w settles at the smallest noise that still covers the experts' diversity, and a theorem shows this condition holds for every network capacity.

The weight rises only where futures branch

Take w̄, the largest weight across action dimensions, and watch it along a rollout. On two real-robot tasks it stays near zero through the committed motion and spikes at the moment a choice is made: which side to push the T from, which block to push. Whichever mode the rollout ends in, the curve is the same up to that moment.

The same weight then schedules three behaviors

Observation schedulew̄ ↑ → remember less

Remember less

Under high uncertainty the policy conditions on a shorter window of past frames, so it is not locked into the route it arrived on. Under low uncertainty it keeps the full history.

Teff = Tm + (To − Tm)(1 − w̄)

Inference schedulew̄ ↑ → think longer

Think longer

The number of denoising steps scales with the uncertainty, at least one and at most a budget Imax. Because most control steps are near-deterministic, the budget is dominated by one-step generation.

k = max(1, ⌈w̄ · Imax⌉)

Execution schedulew̄ ↑ → act cautiously

Act more cautiously

Fewer predicted actions are committed before re-planning when the moment is uncertain; when it is familiar, the policy executes a long chunk open-loop and moves fast.

Eeff = Em + (L − Em)(1 − w̄)

§ 01 · High learning efficiency

AURA learns faster

Generation starts near its target, anchored on the recent action history, and noise is injected only where the local expert spread requires it. The source-to-action mapping is simpler, so it is learned sooner.

Ahead at every budget, most visibly early. On the RoboVerse and LIBERO visuomotor benchmarks AURA stays ahead of all 9 baselines at most training budgets, and the advantage is largest when only a few gradient steps have been taken. The efficiency holds as the backbone scales up: on the π0.5 vision-language-action backbone AURA matches the vanilla policy with half the training steps early on and stays ahead to convergence, and on the ImageWAM world-action backbone it again matches the vanilla policy with half the training steps. The adaptive mechanism thus transfers across all three dominant policy paradigms: visuomotor, VLA and WAM.

Why cautious behavior also raises success. The data itself induces varying learning difficulty. The schedules allocate the policy's effort in proportion to the local difficulty of the learning problem, spending caution only where the data made the mapping hard. That AURA outperforms AURA0, which shares the adaptive noise but keeps behaviors fixed, isolates the contribution of the schedules themselves.

§ 02 · Stable multimodality

Multimodality only when it matters

Several valid solutions to one problem. AURA stays decisive within each mode and turns stochastic only at the branching moment between them, sustaining multimodality far more stably than a fully noised flow policy: a 65 % average gain on real-world tasks.

Counterintuitively, one would expect a fully noised prior to express multimodality most readily. Instead its modes collapse as training proceeds: more steps make the flow policy less multimodal.

§ 03 · Fast and smooth inference

One denoising step where nothing branches

AURA collapses generation to a single step on near-deterministic phases, where conventional generative policies still spend ten to a hundred. Anchoring on the action history keeps successive chunks continuous at their boundaries.

§ 04 · Emergence of novel modes

Behaviors no expert demonstrated

No skill primitives, no scaling, no external planner. At the uncertain moments between sub-tasks AURA looks back less and stays exploratory, so it can stitch together motion learned in different demonstrations: a puzzle assembled, a table tidied, a meal cooked in an order no expert ever showed.

We use the word emergence in a deliberately narrow sense. AURA acquires no new skill and solves no new task. It solves a known task in ways no expert demonstrated, recombining demonstrated segments at whatever granularity the inferred uncertainty rises, from whole sub-tasks down to fragments of a single action. Such emergence is therefore interpretable rather than opaque. On all three long-horizon tasks below the baselines only ever replay a demonstrated order.

Jigsaw Puzzle Franka FR3

Four colored pieces must be moved from an initial layout to a target configuration, a long-horizon task whose sub-assemblies can be ordered in many valid ways. The 100 demonstrations contain only two orders. AURA reproduces both and composes two more at test time: one arises because the two demonstrated orders pass through a briefly identical observation after their second placement, so by shortening its history and re-sampling there AURA is not locked into the route it arrived on; in the other the puzzle is completed in three steps, because placing one piece incidentally carries a neighbouring one into position. Neither baseline shows this capacity. Clips at 2× speed.

Table Tidying Galaxea R1 Lite

A daikon, an ear of corn, a mango and a peach must be cleared into a basket, in any order. The 100 demonstrations cover exactly two orders, 50 each, designed so that after the first two objects the two modes yield similar robot states while requiring different subsequent orders. The flow policy fails on every rollout, at every checkpoint between 25 000 and 200 000 training steps. Clips at 6× speed.

Cooking Simulation

Demonstrated 3 Emergent 2

Light the gas stove, place the pot on it, and add a bell pepper and a broccoli. The 54 demonstrations cover three orders, 18 each, and in every one the two vegetables are adjacent and so are the pot and the stove: the task is always executed as two rigid blocks. Over 150 rollouts across three training seeds AURA succeeds in 16, of which 6 follow one of two orders that appear in no demonstration: one interleaves the vegetables between the pot and the stove, breaking a pair training always kept adjacent; the other reverses the order of the two vegetables. Both recur across seeds. The flow policy succeeds in only 3 of 150, and every success replays a demonstrated order. Clips at 2× speed.

Platforms

Two real robots, both control spaces

Experiments span joint-space and task-space control under both absolute and incremental action modes, plus RoboVerse and Libero in simulation. The π0.5 backbone serves only to show that the adaptive mechanism scales; every behavior-emergence result comes from lightweight visuomotor policies trained from scratch.
